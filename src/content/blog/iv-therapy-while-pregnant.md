That is why so many expecting moms search for IV therapy while pregnant, and why pregnancy IV drips have become one of the most requested mobile IV services on the Treasure Coast.

In fact, IV fluids are one of the most common treatments pregnant women receive in hospitals, especially for severe morning sickness and dehydration.

## Can You Get IV Fluids While Pregnant?

Yes. IV fluids have been used in prenatal care for decades. Hospitals routinely give pregnant patients IV hydration for dehydration, hyperemesis gravidarum (severe morning sickness), heat exhaustion, stomach bugs, and labor support. The fluids themselves, typically normal saline or lactated Ringer's solution, are the same sterile solutions used across all of medicine.

What changes during pregnancy is the level of care around the drip. A pregnancy IV should always be:

* Administered by a licensed registered nurse or other qualified medical professional
* Cleared with your OB, midwife, or prenatal care provider first
* Kept simple and hydration focused, with any added vitamins reviewed for pregnancy safety

Every IV Salt Rejuvenation treatment is administered by a licensed registered nurse under medical director supervision, and we ask expecting clients to get the green light from their prenatal provider before booking.

## Why Dehydration During Pregnancy Is a Bigger Deal

Your blood volume increases by roughly 45 percent during pregnancy, and your body uses water to build amniotic fluid, support the placenta, and regulate temperature. That means your fluid needs go up at the exact moment nausea often makes it harder to drink anything at all.

Common signs of dehydration during pregnancy include:

* Dark yellow urine or urinating less often
* Headaches and dizziness
* Dry mouth and chapped lips
* Fatigue that rest does not fix
* Braxton Hicks contractions that pick up when you have not been drinking enough

Left unchecked, dehydration during pregnancy can contribute to low amniotic fluid, overheating, and preterm contractions. If you have severe symptoms such as confusion, a racing heartbeat, fainting, or contractions that will not stop, skip the mobile IV and contact your OB or go to the hospital. Mobile IV therapy is for mild to moderate dehydration, not emergencies.

## When Expecting Moms Book a Pregnancy IV Drip

### Morning Sickness and Nausea Support

Morning sickness IV therapy is the most common reason pregnant clients call us. When you cannot keep water down, drinking your way back to hydration is nearly impossible. A pregnancy IV drip delivers fluids and electrolytes directly into your bloodstream, bypassing a queasy stomach entirely. Many moms describe feeling noticeably better before the bag is even finished.

For severe, persistent vomiting, <a href="https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/morning-sickness-nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ask your OB about hyperemesis gravidarum</a>. HG is a medical condition that needs physician management, and IV fluids are usually part of that treatment plan. We are happy to be part of your care team, but HG should be diagnosed and directed by your doctor.

Being pregnant on the Treasure Coast in July is its own endurance event. Pregnant bodies run warmer and lose fluids faster, and our humidity does not help. IV hydration can help you rebound after a hot day, a beach outing, or simply existing in a Florida August while growing a human.

A stomach virus that would be a rough day for anyone else can dehydrate a pregnant woman quickly. ## What Goes in a Pregnancy IV? Keeping It Simple

For pregnant clients, less is more. A hydration focused drip built on sterile saline and pregnancy appropriate electrolytes does the heavy lifting. Some additions, like vitamin B6, are commonly recommended in pregnancy for nausea, while other popular IV ingredients found in standard wellness cocktails have not been studied for pregnancy safety and are best skipped.

This is exactly why the provider matters. A registered nurse reviews your health history, your stage of pregnancy, and your OB's guidance before anything goes in the bag. If an ingredient is not clearly appropriate for pregnancy, it stays out. ## Liquid IV While Pregnant vs. an Actual IV Drip

One of the most searched questions on this topic is whether you can drink Liquid I.V. while pregnant. Quick clarification: Liquid I.V. is a powdered electrolyte drink mix you stir into water. Despite the name, it is not an intravenous treatment.

Most sources consider electrolyte drink mixes generally fine in pregnancy in moderation, though some formulas are high in sodium and added sugar, so it is still worth running your favorite brand past your OB. The bigger issue is that a drink mix only works if you can keep it down.

Here is the practical difference:

* Electrolyte drink mixes are a good everyday maintenance tool when nausea is mild and you can drink normally
* An actual pregnancy IV drip delivers fluids and electrolytes directly into your bloodstream with 100 percent absorption, which matters when vomiting or aversions make drinking difficult

If sipping electrolyte water is not cutting it, that is usually the moment an IV makes sense.

## FAQs About IV Therapy While Pregnant

### Can you get IV therapy while pregnant?

Yes, hydration focused IV therapy is commonly used during pregnancy and is routinely given in hospitals for dehydration and severe morning sickness. For elective mobile IV therapy, get clearance from your OB or midwife first and use a service where a licensed nurse administers every treatment.

### Is IV therapy safe in the first trimester?

IV fluids themselves are used in all trimesters in medical settings. Because the first trimester is when nausea peaks and when many moms are most cautious, talk with your prenatal provider about timing and ingredients before booking.

### Does IV therapy help with morning sickness?

IV hydration helps with the dehydration that morning sickness causes, and B6 is commonly recommended for pregnancy nausea. It is a support tool, not a cure. If vomiting is severe or constant, ask your doctor about <a href="https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/12232-hyperemesis-gravidarum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">hyperemesis gravidarum</a>.

### Can I drink Liquid I.V. while pregnant instead of getting a drip?

Electrolyte drink mixes are generally considered fine in moderation during pregnancy, but confirm with your OB. They only help if you can keep fluids down. When you cannot, an actual IV drip delivers hydration directly into your bloodstream.

### How long does a pregnancy IV drip take?

Most treatments take about 45 to 60 minutes from setup to finish, all in the comfort of your home.
