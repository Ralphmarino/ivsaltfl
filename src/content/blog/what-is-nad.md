NAD+ has gone from biochemistry textbooks to celebrity wellness routines, and with that jump came a lot of big promises. So here is an honest NAD+ 101 from the team at [IV Salt Rejuvenation](/): what NAD+ is, what it does in your body, what the research supports so far, and what it does not.

> **Quick answer:** NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every cell of your body. It is essential for converting food into energy and supporting DNA repair, and your levels naturally decline with age. Research confirms that supplements and infusions can raise NAD+ levels, but studies proving benefits like slowed aging are still in progress. NAD+ is not related to Ozempic and is not a weight loss drug.

## What Is NAD+?

NAD+ stands for nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide. It is a small molecule found in every living cell, and without it you could not turn food into energy, move your muscles, or stay alive. It is not a vitamin, a hormone, or a drug. It is a coenzyme, a helper molecule that makes thousands of chemical reactions in your body possible.

NAD exists in two forms that work as a pair. NAD+ is the "empty" form, ready to pick up electrons during chemical reactions, and NADH is the "full" form carrying electrons your cells use to make energy. The two constantly shuttle back and forth, which is how energy keeps flowing inside your cells.

Your body builds NAD+ from precursors, mainly forms of vitamin B3 (niacin) and the amino acid tryptophan, found in foods like poultry, fish, whole grains, legumes, eggs, and dairy.

## What NAD+ Does in the Body

According to the <a href="https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/nad-nicotinamide-adenine-dinucleotide" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cleveland Clinic, NAD's most important job</a> is helping convert the food you eat into usable energy, a process that happens largely in your mitochondria, the power plants of your cells. Beyond energy production, NAD+ supports:

- **DNA repair.** NAD+ fuels the enzymes that fix damage to your genetic material.
- **Metabolism.** It drives the chemical reactions that break down nutrients.
- **Cell protection.** It helps limit harmful byproducts that build up when cells are under stress.
- **Muscle, brain, and heart function.** These energy-hungry tissues rely heavily on NAD+.

## Why Everyone Is Talking About NAD+ (the Aging Connection)

Here is the fact driving the entire NAD+ trend: your NAD+ levels naturally decline as you age. Some research suggests the drop can be substantial by later adulthood, and lower NAD+ levels have been linked to age-related changes like muscle weakness, fatigue, metabolic issues, and cognitive decline.

That link is real and well documented. The open question, and it is genuinely still open, is whether restoring NAD+ levels in humans reverses or slows those changes. Which brings us to what people use it for.

## What Is NAD+ Used For?

People seek out NAD+ therapy, whether as an IV infusion, injection, or oral supplement, for goals like:

- Energy and fighting fatigue
- Mental clarity and focus
- Healthy aging and cellular repair support
- Recovery after intense exertion or long weeks
- General wellness optimization

NAD+ infusions deliver the coenzyme directly into your bloodstream, bypassing digestion entirely. That matters more for NAD+ than for most nutrients, because NAD+ itself is poorly absorbed when swallowed, which is why oral products use precursors instead (more on that below).

## NAD+ Benefits: What the Research Actually Shows

We promised honesty, so here is the research picture in three tiers.

**Well established:** NAD+ is essential to energy production and DNA repair, and levels decline with age. None of that is controversial. It is standard biochemistry, confirmed across decades of research.

**Demonstrated in studies:** Supplementing with NAD+ precursors reliably raises NAD+ levels in the blood. Human trials, including studies lasting up to two years, have also consistently found NAD+ precursor supplementation to be safe at studied doses.

**Still being studied:** The exciting outcomes, more energy, sharper thinking, slower aging, better metabolic health, have strong support in animal studies but limited or inconclusive evidence in humans so far. <a href="https://health.clevelandclinic.org/nad-supplement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cleveland Clinic experts note that researchers are actively studying NAD+</a> for cardiovascular health, neurologic conditions, and metabolic health, but stress that evidence for these uses remains early. Some human studies show benefits, others show none, and scientists are still working out who benefits most.

In plain terms: the biology is real, raising your levels is achievable, and the payoff question is still being answered. Anyone who tells you NAD+ is proven to reverse aging is ahead of the science. Anyone who tells you it is snake oil is ignoring a serious and active research field. The truth is in the middle, and we would rather you hear that from us.

## Is NAD+ Like Ozempic?

No. This question comes up constantly because both became famous around the same time, but they have nothing in common.

Ozempic is a brand name for semaglutide, a prescription medication in the GLP-1 class that mimics a gut hormone to regulate blood sugar and appetite. It is a drug, prescribed by a doctor, primarily for type 2 diabetes and weight management.

NAD+ is a naturally occurring coenzyme your body already makes and uses in every cell. It does not act on appetite hormones, and it is not a weight loss treatment. While NAD+ plays a role in metabolism at the cellular level, no research supports NAD+ therapy as a weight loss method, and any provider marketing it that way is overreaching.

If weight management is your goal, that is a conversation for your physician, not an IV menu.

## What Is the Downside of Taking NAD+?

A few honest downsides to know before you book or buy:

- **Infusion side effects.** NAD+ IVs are known for causing temporary nausea, flushing, chest pressure, or muscle cramping if the drip runs too fast. These effects resolve when the rate is slowed, which is exactly why NAD+ should only be administered by a licensed medical professional who monitors you and adjusts the drip.
- **Time.** Unlike a standard 45 to 60 minute hydration drip, NAD+ infusions typically take one to several hours because of that slower, safer rate.
- **Cost.** NAD+ is one of the more expensive drips on any menu, and it is not covered by insurance.
- **Unproven long-term benefits.** As covered above, you may be paying for outcomes research has not confirmed yet. We think you should know that going in.
- **Not for everyone.** If you are pregnant, nursing, managing a chronic condition, or taking medications, talk to your doctor first. Long-term safety data at high doses is still limited.

## Do NAD+ Supplements Really Work?

Oral NAD+ supplements come with a wrinkle: NAD+ itself is poorly absorbed through the digestive tract. That is why most products contain precursors instead, like nicotinamide riboside (NR) or nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN), which your body converts into NAD+.

Do they work? Partially proven: human trials show oral precursors do raise blood NAD+ levels, modestly at typical doses and more at higher ones. What remains unproven is whether that translates into the energy, longevity, and health benefits on the label. Supplement quality also varies widely since the industry is lightly regulated.

The case for IV delivery is about the delivery itself: an infusion puts NAD+ directly into your bloodstream with none lost to digestion, administered in measured doses by a medical professional. Whether IV or oral fits your goals better is a fair conversation to have with your nurse or doctor.
